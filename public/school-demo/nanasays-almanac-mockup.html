Chapter the first
On the matter of brochures, prospectuses, and the gentle art of saying nothing

Every school shines in its own prospectus.

The trouble with researching a British boarding school is that the school in question would rather you didn't. Not in any sinister way — only that the documents the school does hand you tend to be photographs of summer sports days and three sentences of mission-statement Latin.

Nanasays is what happens when someone reads the rest of it — the inspection report, the fee schedule, the safeguarding policy, the boarding-house handbook — and writes down what the brochure does not say.
